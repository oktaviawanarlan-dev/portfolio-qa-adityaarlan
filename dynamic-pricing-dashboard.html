Dynamic Pricing Dashboard

Internal hotel pricing-management platform — QA case study covering pricing accuracy, scheduling, access control, bulk operations, and downstream price distribution.

Overview

Dynamic Pricing Dashboard is an internal hotel pricing and revenue-management platform covering property pricing configuration, room-category pricing, occupancy-based pricing, minimum and maximum pricing boundaries, fixed and dynamic pricing configurations, percentage-based price adjustments, scheduled pricing changes, room-plan configuration, final selling-price calculation, bulk operations, user permissions, pricing history, and external price distribution — across development, staging, and production environments.

My Role

Responsible for quality ownership of pricing-critical workflows — validating that pricing calculations, schedules, access control, and downstream distribution behave as intended. A pricing defect here carries direct business and revenue impact, so testing prioritised accuracy, scope, and data integrity before anything else.

Testing covered functional, regression, exploratory, and integration testing, API validation, boundary-value and decision-table analysis, role and access testing, data-integrity validation, defect investigation, fix verification, and release validation — collaborating with developers and product stakeholders across the development, staging, and production environments.

Bug Case Study

Two examples of defect investigation on an internal pricing platform — identifying not just that something broke, but where the enforcement gap was.

Case Study 01

Pricing Schedule Applied Outside Intended Conditions

Problem A scheduled pricing adjustment produces an unexpected selling price for a specific combination of date, room category, occupancy, or configuration.
QA Challenge Verify that pricing rules affect only their intended scope and do not modify unrelated dates, rooms, occupancy levels, or properties.
Investigation Trace the calculation through each stage to locate where behavior diverges from the expected pricing rule: Pricing Configuration → Request / Submitted Data → Backend Response → Pricing Schedule → Final Selling Price. QA compares the expected and actual result at every stage to narrow down the responsible layer.
Outcome The responsible layer is identified and the fix is verified against the original scenario plus surrounding boundary and regression cases.
Case Study 02

Access Control / Data Scope Validation

Problem A user with restricted access appears able to reach or perform an operation outside their expected property or permission scope.
QA Challenge Verify authorization beyond simple UI visibility — the restriction must hold at the request and data level, not only on screen.
Investigation Test across: normal UI navigation, direct page access, restricted operations, different permission levels, different property assignments, and backend authorization behavior.
Outcome QA ensures access restrictions are consistently enforced both in the user interface and at the request/backend level.
