Overview

Direct Channel is a customer-facing hotel booking platform covering search, room/plan selection, pricing and promotions, payment, booking confirmation, and cancellation, across development, staging, and production environments.

My role was quality ownership for this product — testing not just "pages" but the reliability of the entire booking experience end-to-end.

My Role

Responsible for validating the reliability of the end-to-end hotel booking experience, from search through payment, confirmation, and cancellation — collaborating with developers and product stakeholders to identify risk, design test scenarios, investigate defects, and verify releases across dev, staging, and production.

Testing was supported by Qase.io for test case management, JIRA for defect and release tracking, Postman for API validation, DBeaver for database verification, and AWS CloudWatch alongside Datadog for log-based investigation of live issues.

Case Study 01

Payment Method Availability Validation Gap

Problem When a payment method's availability status changed on the backend (e.g. set to "maintenance" or "unavailable"), the customer-facing behavior didn't consistently reflect that status across different payment methods.
QA Challenge Verify that "unavailable" and "maintenance" states are enforced consistently — both visually (UI disabled with a message) and functionally (booking creation blocked) — regardless of which payment method is affected.
Investigation For one payment method, the UI correctly greyed out the option under "maintenance" status, but still allowed selection and successful booking under "unavailable" status. For a second payment method, the UI didn't grey out or message the user under either status, and bookings could be completed in both cases.
Outcome Flagged that unavailable/maintenance states needed identical enforcement across all payment methods — blocking selection on the frontend, plus a backend validation preventing booking creation, modification, or cancellation during the affected window.
Case Study 02

Verification Link Behavior After Account Switch

Problem A verification link sent during an email-update flow led to a blank page under a specific multi-account sequence.
QA Challenge Validate link behavior beyond the simple case — under realistic scenarios where a user switches accounts before completing verification.
Steps User logs in with Account A, updates email to a new address, logs out, logs into a different Account C, then opens the updated inbox and clicks the original verification link. Result: the link directed to an empty page — the flow only had defined behavior for "not logged in" and "logged in as the original user," not "logged in as a different account."
Outcome Flagged as an undefined edge case for product/engineering to define, rather than assuming a fix — since expected behavior hadn't been specified.
